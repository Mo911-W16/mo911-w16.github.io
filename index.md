## 🎯 What Is dotpals?

AI coding agents are wonderful at sounding confident. They say things like "All tests pass!" and "Fixed the bug!" — and sometimes that's true, and sometimes it isn't.

dotpals watches those claims. It reads the actual output your agent produces, checks the test results that come back, and lets you know when a "pass" was faked or skipped. Think of it as a small, cheerful fact-checker that lives on your desktop.

It appears as a compact **pal** and a slim **notch** at the top of your screen — always visible, never in the way.

## 🔍 Catching Faked Test Results

This is the heart of dotpals, and it's worth understanding even if you never write a line of code.

An AI agent might say:

> "Tests are passing — everything is ready."

But the actual output might show that the tests were skipped, that a test file was quietly deleted, or that the test command never ran at all. Without dotpals, you'd have to scroll back through pages of output to notice. Most people don't. That's how broken code reaches real users.

dotpals compares what your agent *claims* with what the output *actually contains*:

- A real, completed test run is reported as a pass.
- A skipped, blocked, or missing test run is flagged as suspicious.
- A test command that was never executed is called out plainly.

You get a simple, honest verdict. No jargon, no ambiguity.
